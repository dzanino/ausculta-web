FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does Ausculta use the microphone?

No. Ausculta is an atlas of recorded examples: it plays sounds that are part of the app and does not ask for access to the microphone. It picks up, records and measures nothing.

Is it a medical device?

No. Ausculta is an educational atlas for learning auscultation. It examines no one, measures nothing, does not diagnose and does not replace an examination by a doctor. Always consult a doctor about health concerns.

Do I need headphones?

No, the examples also play through the speaker. Heart, lung and bowel sounds are quiet, though, and contain many low tones that a small speaker plays weakly. In headphones (any will do, wired or Bluetooth) you hear more detail. If you cannot hear an example, turn up the volume; some sounds, such as a third heart sound or fine crackles, are quiet in the original recording too.

How does the quiz work?

The quiz plays an atlas example and you pick the type of sound from up to four names, for example normal heart sounds, a systolic murmur or wheezes. You see right away whether your answer was correct, along with the textbook description, and at the end of the round the number of correct answers. Only your answers are scored. Without Ausculta Pro the quiz uses the free examples, with Pro all of them.

What is Compare two?

Two atlas examples side by side (A and B), each with its description. Play one, then the other, and notice the difference. It suits sounds that are easy to confuse, such as fine and coarse crackles, or wheezes and rhonchi.

What do “Heard X of Y” and “Not yet heard” mean?

The app remembers on the device which examples you have already played. “Heard X of Y” shows how many examples in a section you have heard, and the “Not yet heard” filter shows only the rest. This stays on your device.

Where do the atlas examples come from?

They are short excerpts from open research databases: CirCor DigiScope and EPHNOGRAM (PhysioNet, ODC-By 1.0 licence), KAUH (Mendeley Data, CC BY 4.0), SPRSound (CC BY 4.0), HF_Lung_V1 (CC BY 4.0), HLS-CMDS (recordings from a clinical teaching manikin, CC BY 4.0), JUST Heart Sound Regurgitation (Mendeley Data, CC BY 4.0), SMART-Ped (Zenodo, CC BY 4.0), and bowel sounds from Bowel sounds signal (Mansour, figshare, CC BY 4.0) and Bowel Sounds Audio Files (Rulli et al., figshare, CC BY 4.0); one after-meal example comes from the freesound.org sound library (contact microphone, CC BY 4.0). In the app every example shows its source and licence; the full citations are under About, authorship & references › Atlas: sources and licences. The excerpts were shortened, processed and evened out in loudness for Ausculta. The database authors are not connected with the app and do not endorse it. More in the technical info.

What does Ausculta Pro unlock and what is free?

Free: 12 of the 35 atlas sounds, the quiz and Compare two with the free examples, all 4 ECG examples with heart sound and the whole guide. Ausculta Pro is a one-time in-app purchase (the App Store shows the price in your local currency), not a subscription. It unlocks the other 23 atlas sounds, which then also appear in the quiz and in Compare two. Pro examples are marked in the atlas; tapping a locked example opens the Pro offer, which is also in Settings.

How do I restore my purchase on a new iPhone or after reinstalling?

Open Settings in the app and tap Restore purchases. Pro is restored with no further payment on every device signed in to the same Apple Account. Contributions are not restored because they unlock nothing.

What are the contributions under Support development?

Three voluntary one-time contributions to the development and maintenance of Ausculta. They unlock nothing and are not subscriptions: the app works the same with or without them. Thank you to everyone who contributes.

Where can I find the guide to auscultation with a stethoscope?

In the app, in the Guide tab: bell and diaphragm, auscultation sites of the heart and lungs, the four abdominal quadrants and how long to listen. A short version is in the guide on this website.

Where to start

Start with normal heart sounds at different sites and normal breathing, and only then move on to murmurs and added sounds. Listen to each example several times with its description, then test yourself in the quiz.

Headphones

Any headphones will do, wired or Bluetooth. The examples also play through the speaker, but you hear more in headphones.

Repetition

Come back to the examples after a few days. The “Not yet heard” filter shows what you have not heard yet.
