FAQ

Frequently asked questions

I hear nothing

Connect AirPods or headphones and check the "Hear the probe in headphones" switch. Press the probe more firmly on bare skin and hold it still for 10 s: the automatic gain compensates for a weak signal.

Why is the sound muted when I have no headphones?

Otherwise the microphone sound would play from the iPhone speaker and be picked up by the microphone again, causing a squeal. That is why listening stays muted until headphones are connected. Connect AirPods, wired or other headphones.

It squeals

The sound is coming from the speaker: turn on "Mute without headphones" in Settings or connect headphones.

Why is there no AirPod probe in the EU?

The AirPod probe needs the high-quality AirPods microphone link offered by iOS 26 with AirPods Pro 2 / Pro 3 or AirPods 4. Apple does not make it available to apps in EU countries. Without it the sound from the AirPod would be transmitted at phone-call quality (narrow band, often muffled or choppy), which is not good enough for listening. So the AirPod probe is not offered there and the iPhone probe is used, which is the main probe anyway: AirPods then simply play the sound back.

How do I make a bell?

You need a small cup that seals against the skin and holds little air. The easiest is a shallow PET-bottle cap: place its open side on the skin and bring the iPhone microphone (or the AirPod stem tip) to the small hole in it, sealing around the stem with modelling clay or tape. A 1 cm ring cut from a syringe, a silicone ear tip turned inside out, or a palm cupped into a bowl also works. A whole long syringe barrel hardly transmits any sound. Pictures are in the guide.

The sound in my ear is suddenly mono and "telephone-like"

This is the Bluetooth hands-free profile: when the app uses the AirPods microphone, older AirPods also switch their output to call-quality mono. Solution: use the iPhone probe mode, or AirPods Pro 2/3 or AirPods 4 on iOS 26 outside the EU.

It sometimes goes quiet in my ear

Check Settings › Bluetooth › AirPods: turn off Conversation Awareness and Adaptive mode, keep Noise Cancellation. A short dip right after a bump is intended: Ausculta protects your ears when fingers or the stem knock against the probe.

iOS uses the wrong AirPod as the microphone

Settings › Bluetooth › ⓘ next to AirPods › Microphone › Always Left / Always Right. Choose the side you hold on the chest.

What do the alerts (probe movement, hand tremor, noise) mean?

They are messages about sound quality: Ausculta reports that the signal is currently disturbed by probe movement, hand tremor, speech, room noise or poor contact. They say nothing about your health. Rest your hand on your body, hold the probe lightly and still, choose a quiet room and try again.

It rustles about ten times a second

That is the tremor of your own hand: a microphone pressed straight on the skin hears every tiny shake. Rest your hand or forearm on your body, hold lightly, use a bell made from a cap – or lie down and let the iPhone rest freely on your chest. Ausculta recognises the tremor and alerts you.

With the AirPod probe I hear single thumps

These are finger movements on the AirPod or the stem rubbing on the skin or cap. Keep the stem tip firmly in the cap, the hand resting on the chest and the fingers still. Ausculta briefly mutes such bumps and alerts you.

Is it a medical device?

No. Ausculta is an educational aid for learning to listen. It measures nothing, does not diagnose and does not replace an examination by a doctor. Always consult a doctor about health concerns.

Where are my recordings?

In the Recordings tab, physically in the app folder (Files › On My iPhone › Ausculta). You can share them with the Share WAV icon.

Best sound

iPhone probe + any headphones. The iPhone microphone records the full 48 kHz band without voice processing.

Where to start

Bell (low sounds), cardiac apex (5th intercostal space, midclavicular line), 10 s of stillness. Then the diaphragm on the lung bases at the back.

Learning

Compare what you hear with the sound examples from an auscultation textbook. Ausculta assesses nothing, you do.
