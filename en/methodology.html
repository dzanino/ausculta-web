How Ausculta works

A short description of what happens to the sound from the microphone to the headphones, and where the atlas sounds come from. Ausculta is an educational aid: it amplifies, filters and displays sound. It measures nothing and assesses nothing.

Signal path

From microphone to headphones

Capture

The main probe is the iPhone: the bottom microphone at full quality (48 kHz), without voice processing. The AirPod probe is offered where iOS provides a high-quality microphone link (iOS 26, AirPods Pro 2 / Pro 3, AirPods 4, outside the EU). It is not offered in the EU; use the iPhone probe there.

Unfiltered copy

While recording, the microphone sound is also taken before the filter and gain and saved as a separate WAV next to the recording. The recording you heard stays filtered. You can turn off saving the copy in Settings; sharing it is part of Pro.

Band filter

The bell passes the 20–220 Hz band (low sounds), the diaphragm the 100–1200 Hz band (higher sounds). The filter emphasises what one learns to hear when listening and suppresses the rest.

Your own band (Pro)

Instead of the Bell, Diaphragm and Wide filters you can set your own band: the lower edge in the 20–400 Hz range and the upper edge in the 150–2500 Hz range. While your own band is on, noise reduction works without the quiet-room calibration.

Amplification

Automatic or manual gain evens out a weak signal. A limiter in the gain stage protects your ears from sudden bumps.

Noise reduction

After calibration in silence, Ausculta subtracts the ambient noise and dampens bumps caused by probe movement.

Display

The sound waveform and frequency spectrum in real time. They are pictures of sound, as in audio editing software, not health values.

Listening and recordings

You hear the processed sound in your headphones. Recordings are saved as WAV with the listening site and a note, and you can play back and share them any time. The free version keeps 3 recordings, Pro an unlimited number.

Signal quality

Interference alerts

Ausculta reports when the sound is disturbed by probe movement, hand tremor, speech, room noise or poor contact with the skin. These are messages about sound quality, so you know to hold the probe more steadily or press it better. They say nothing about your health.

Atlas

How the atlas sounds are prepared

The atlas contains short excerpts from open teaching databases. Every excerpt went through the same processing, so the examples are comfortable to listen to and compare.

Choosing the excerpt

A 10–12 s section is taken from the recording, without speech, crying, rubbing or a saturated (clipped) signal.

Filtering

The DC offset and deep rumble are removed: a 20 Hz high-pass for heart sounds and 80 Hz for breathing. The filters are zero-phase, so the sound is not shifted in time.

Resampling

The examples are stored at 8000 samples per second, 16 bit, mono. That is enough for heart and breath sounds and keeps the app small.

Loudness

All examples are brought to the same average loudness. Peaks are never clipped: where clipping would be a risk, the example stays a little quieter instead.

Fade in and out

The start and end of each example fade in and out smoothly (100 ms), so playback does not start or end with a click.

ECG + sound

ECG and heart sound on one time axis

The examples come from the EPHNOGRAM database, in which the ECG and the heart sound were recorded together in adults at rest. Ausculta itself does not record an ECG. You see both traces one above the other on a shared time axis, and during playback a time marker moves along them, so you can follow how the heart sounds come after the electrical activation. The heart sound has the mains hum (50 Hz and its multiples) removed, is limited to the band up to 1 kHz and is stored at 4000 samples per second. The ECG is in the 0.5–40 Hz band at 250 samples per second. All filters are zero-phase, so the traces are not shifted in time against each other.

Nothing is measured or assessed in the examples: Ausculta shows no intervals and no numbers and does not interpret the ECG trace. The shape of the ECG depends on where the electrodes were placed.

Limits

What Ausculta does not do

Ausculta shows no health values, does not recognise or name sounds in your recordings, produces no diagnoses and gives no recommendations. It is not a medical device, it measures nothing, does not diagnose and does not replace an examination by a doctor. You compare what you hear yourself, for example with the reference sounds in the atlas or in a textbook. Always consult a doctor about health concerns.
