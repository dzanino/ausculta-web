Technical info

How Ausculta works

A short description of what happens to the sound from the microphone to the headphones. Ausculta is an educational aid: it amplifies, filters and displays sound. It measures nothing and assesses nothing.

Signal path

From microphone to headphones

Capture

The main probe is the iPhone: the bottom microphone at full quality (48 kHz), without voice processing. The AirPod probe is offered only where iOS provides a high-quality microphone link (iOS 26, AirPods Pro 2 / Pro 3, AirPods 4, outside the EU).

Band filter

The bell passes the 20–220 Hz band (low sounds), the diaphragm the 100–1200 Hz band (higher sounds). The filter emphasises what one learns to hear when listening and suppresses the rest.

Amplification

Automatic or manual gain evens out a weak signal. A limiter in the gain stage protects your ears from sudden bumps.

Noise reduction

After calibration in silence, Ausculta subtracts the ambient noise and dampens bumps caused by probe movement.

Display

The sound waveform and frequency spectrum in real time. They are pictures of sound, as in audio editing software, not health values.

Listening and recordings

You hear the processed sound in your headphones. Recordings are saved as WAV with the listening site and a note, and you can play back and share them any time.

Signal quality

Interference alerts

Ausculta reports when the sound is disturbed by probe movement, hand tremor, speech, room noise or poor contact with the skin. These are messages about sound quality, so you know to hold the probe more steadily or press it better. They say nothing about your health.

Limits

What Ausculta does not do

Ausculta shows no health values, does not recognise or name sounds, produces no diagnoses and gives no recommendations. It is not a medical device, it measures nothing, does not diagnose and does not replace an examination by a doctor. You compare what you hear yourself, for example with textbook examples. Always consult a doctor about health concerns.
