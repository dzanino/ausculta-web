Ausculta is an educational tool for learning to listen. It amplifies and filters the sound picked up by your iPhone, and you hear it in your AirPods or headphones. On screen you see the waveform and spectrum, you save recordings and compare what you hear with the reference sounds in the atlas, which come from open teaching databases. Free to download.

Listening from your pocket

Learn to listen with what you already have in your pocket. Put the iPhone's bottom microphone into a bell on your chest, put your AirPods in, and you hear an amplified sound of the heart and breathing. Optionally… …where iOS allows it, an AirPod can serve as the probe too: one in the ear, the other with its stem in a bottle cap. Ausculta amplifies and filters the sound, it assesses nothing: you compare it yourself.

Two ways

Where is the probe?

iPhone probe main

The iPhone's bottom microphone in a bell on the chest; AirPods or any headphones only play back, in full quality and without voice processing.

AirPod probe

One AirPod in the ear, the other with its stem tip on the chest. It is offered where iOS provides a high-quality microphone link: iOS 26 with AirPods Pro 2, Pro 3 or AirPods 4, outside the EU. Elsewhere use the iPhone probe; the AirPods then simply play it back.

Features

What Ausculta offers

Amplified listening

The microphone sound is amplified and filtered so you hear it better in AirPods or headphones. The Heart filter (20–220 Hz, like a bell) is for low sounds, Lungs (100–1200 Hz, like a diaphragm) for higher ones and Wide for the full band.

Heart and lungs

Learn to tell sounds apart at different places on the chest. Ausculta does not assess them: you listen and compare them yourself with the reference sounds in the atlas or in a textbook.

Atlas of reference sounds

Textbook heart and lung sounds from open teaching databases (PhysioNet and others), each with its source and licence. You compare them yourself, by ear: Ausculta does not compare your recording with them.

ECG and heart sound

Examples from an open database in which the ECG and the heart sound were recorded together. You see them one above the other on one time axis and, during playback, follow how the heart sounds come after the electrical activation. Ausculta itself does not record an ECG and measures nothing in the examples.

Live waveform and spectrum

The sound waveform and frequency spectrum in real time, as in audio editing software. Calibration in silence and noise reduction.

Interference alerts

The app reports probe movement, hand tremor, speech, noise or poor contact so you know the signal is disturbed. It is a message about sound quality, not about your health.

Recordings

Full-quality WAV with the listening site and notes. An unfiltered copy (the microphone sound before the filter) is saved with every recording for your own archive. Play back and share with one tap.

Privacy

The app uses no server, analytics or accounts. Sound and recordings stay on your iPhone.

Guide

Getting started

iPhone probe

The easiest start. Put the iPhone's bottom microphone into a bell on your chest and your headphones in your ears. The sound is full quality and without voice processing.

A bell from household items

A shallow PET-bottle cap, a 1 cm ring cut from a syringe, a silicone ear tip turned inside out – or a cupped palm. What matters is little air inside and a sealed hole around the stem (modelling clay, tape). A whole long syringe barrel hardly transmits any sound.

A grip without rustling

With the AirPod probe the microphone is at the stem tip. A: stem tip in the hole of a cap resting on the skin. B: tip directly on the skin, palm as a shield. The hand rests on the chest and the fingers stay still – every touch on the AirPod is loud in the probe.

Which AirPod goes where

For the AirPod probe only. Acoustically it makes no difference. Right-handed users hold the right AirPod and wear the left in the ear. In Settings › Bluetooth › AirPods › Microphone choose "Always Right" and turn on noise cancellation on the side in the ear. AirPods setup in the app walks you through it.

Where to listen

A aortic, P pulmonic, E Erb's point, T tricuspid, M apex. Lungs: apices, bases, back. Always compare the left and right side, and compare what you hear with the reference sounds in the atlas.

A clean signal

A quiet room, the probe on the skin, the hand resting on the body and 10–20 s without moving. Do the calibration in silence once. The automatic gain handles the rest.

Pricing

Free and Ausculta Pro

Ausculta is a free download. Ausculta Pro is a one-time in-app purchase, not a subscription.

Free

Listening with the iPhone or AirPod probe, the Bell, Diaphragm and Wide filters, waveform and spectrum, calibration, the guide, the ECG and sound examples and the basic atlas. You can keep 3 recordings.

Ausculta Pro

Unlimited recordings, sharing of unfiltered copies, your own filter band and the full atlas. One-time purchase; the App Store shows the price in your local currency.
